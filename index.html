Hello, I'm David

Better estimates for small areas, from survey data.

I am a Ph.D. student in Statistics at Iowa State University, advised by Dr. Emily J. Berg.

I work on small area estimation: getting reliable estimates for districts, counties and other groups where the survey sample is small. My work covers zero-inflated log-normal models and mixed-effects machine learning. I do most of it in R.

I earned my B.Sc. in Statistics from Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology in Kumasi, Ghana.

What I work on

Research

I am a Graduate Research Assistant with Dr. Emily J. Berg (since Aug 2023). I build and test small area estimation methods using simulation studies.

Mixed effects random forests for small area estimation

My M.S. thesis. Random forests combined with random area effects to estimate area means when the response is log-normal.

M.S. thesis

Super learner for small area estimation

Combines several machine learning methods (random forests, gradient boosting, SVR and a linear model) inside a mixed model, tested on mean income for 70 Austrian districts (EU-SILC).

CurrentR

Zero-inflated log-normal models

A model for survey variables that have many exact zeros and skewed positive values, such as income components and expenditures.

Papers

Manuscripts in Preparation

  • Small area estimation with a super learner: combining machine learning methods within the mixed model

    D. Ayikambey and E. J. Berg

    Instead of choosing one machine learning method, we combine several of them inside a mixed model and let the data decide how much each one counts.

  • Small area estimation for survey variables with many zeros: a zero-inflated log-normal approach

    D. Ayikambey and E. J. Berg

    A model for variables that are zero for many people and skewed for the rest, used to estimate area means and their uncertainty.

Selected graduate work

Projects

Dirichlet regression for crop acreage shares

Modeled California crop acreage shares as one compositional system. Fixed weak identification with profile likelihood and built confidence bands with the multivariate delta method.

Growth curves with AR(1) errors

Fit Gompertz and AR(1)-error models to 50 years of harvested-acreage data, using composite likelihood, parametric bootstrap intervals and a 50,000-replicate simulation.
